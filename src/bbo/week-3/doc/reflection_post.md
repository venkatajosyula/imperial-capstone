# Week 3 Reflection

How has your query strategy changed from earlier rounds? Do you rely more on model predictions, or are you still exploring new regions? Do you tune hyperparameters or rely on heuristics?
The Week 3 strategy shifts from uniform heuristic blending toward evidence weighted regional modelling. Earlier rounds used a fixed top 3 blend as the primary selector. With additional observations now available, the same blend is retained as a baseline, but regional quality is interpreted using a provisional SVM framing in which observations are separated into higher and lower performance sets. Recent outputs motivate this shift: Function 5 improved substantially to 836.08 and Function 2 improved to 0.6416, while Function 7 declined to 2.406 and Function 8 softened to 9.8057, indicating that function specific adjustments are preferable to a single global rule.

How do you balance exploration against exploitation? Do you focus more on areas known to perform well, or are you still sampling from untested regions?
The balance remains exploitation dominant with constrained local exploration. High performing regions receive greater weight because the weekly budget is limited and delayed exploitation is costly. This is most evident in Functions 2, 4, 5, and 6, where the latest outputs improved after moving toward stronger historical regions. Exploration is retained through controlled perturbations in functions with weaker stability, particularly Functions 3, 7, and 8, to limit local lock in while preserving directional search efficiency.

How would SVMs change your approach? Could you use a soft-margin SVM to classify high vs low performance regions? Would a kernel SVM help if the response surface is non-linear?
SVMs formalise regional selection and were implemented this week as a parallel query generator alongside the heuristic baseline. Observations above the 50th percentile are labelled high performance; the remainder low performance. A soft margin RBF SVC (C=1.0) is fitted on this labelled dataset and scored against 10,000 random candidates; the point with the highest decision function value becomes the suggested query. All eight functions produced SVM queries that differed from the heuristic, demonstrating that the classifier builds its own view of the high performance region rather than simply deferring to the top ranked historical point. For example, Function 5 shifted from 0.219-0.847-0.849-0.877 under the heuristic to 0.724-0.066-0.863-0.836 under the SVM, reflecting the SVM's preference for a different region of that four dimensional space. The RBF kernel is appropriate for Functions 6 to 8, where interaction effects are likely non additive and a linear boundary would misclassify regions near curved performance contours.

What limitations of your current model become apparent as data grows? Is it overfitting? Do any features or dimensions emerge as irrelevant?
As data grows, two limitations become clearer. First, the weighted blend does not quantify uncertainty, so it may over concentrate on recent maxima and overfit local structures. Second, it assumes uniform feature relevance, which is unlikely in higher dimensions. The mixed behaviour in Functions 7 and 8 despite prior strong outputs suggests sensitivity to interaction effects that are not captured by linear blending. Without feature screening or margin based regional classification, weakly informative dimensions may dilute query quality.

How does this black-box set-up prepare you to think like a data scientist when faced with incomplete knowledge in other projects?
The black box setting enforces disciplined decisions under uncertainty. It requires iterative hypothesis revision, constrained experimentation, and explicit tradeoff management between immediate gain and information acquisition. These practices transfer directly to real projects with partial observability, expensive labels, and distribution shift. The workflow also reinforces reproducibility through consistent query generation, traceable assumptions, and post round diagnostic reflection.
